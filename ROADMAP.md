# Complete Roadmap

## The learning cycle

Every level uses the same cycle:

1. **Understand:** study short, level-appropriate language.
2. **Notice:** identify useful words, grammar, sounds, and text structure.
3. **Practice:** do focused exercises.
4. **Produce:** speak and write about real topics.
5. **Get feedback:** find repeated errors.
6. **Review:** revisit material after 1, 7, and 30 days.
7. **Test:** complete a real task without notes.

Spend approximately 40% of study time understanding input, 40% producing
language, and 20% reviewing and correcting.

## Phase 1: strong A1 → A2

**Suggested duration:** 12–16 weeks  
**Suggested hours:** 120–160

Focus on:

- essential sentence patterns and questions;
- the most useful everyday vocabulary;
- slow, clear speech about familiar topics;
- short conversations without translating every sentence;
- simple connected writing.

Main projects:

- a three-minute personal introduction;
- a recorded description of a normal day;
- role-plays: café, shop, doctor, directions, hotel;
- a 100-word message or story.

Detailed plan: [`levels/A1-A2.md`](levels/A1-A2.md)

## Phase 2: A2 → B1

**Suggested duration:** 20–24 weeks  
**Suggested hours:** 180–240

Focus on:

- connecting sentences into stories and explanations;
- understanding the main point of clear standard speech;
- travel, work, study, relationships, health, and media;
- expressing opinions and giving reasons;
- reading graded readers and short authentic texts.

Main projects:

- a five-minute story about a past experience;
- solve a travel problem through role-play;
- read and review a graded reader;
- write a 150-word email or opinion.

Detailed plan: [`levels/A2-B1.md`](levels/A2-B1.md)

## Phase 3: B1 → B2

**Suggested duration:** 24–32 weeks  
**Suggested hours:** 220–300

Focus on:

- longer authentic podcasts, videos, articles, and fiction;
- spontaneous conversation with less preparation;
- explaining and defending opinions;
- collocations, phrasal verbs, register, and natural phrasing;
- clear essays, reports, and professional messages.

Main projects:

- a 10-minute presentation plus questions;
- a structured debate;
- four authentic article summaries;
- a 250-word essay revised after feedback.

Detailed plan: [`levels/B1-B2.md`](levels/B1-B2.md)

## Phase 4: B2 → C1

**Suggested duration:** 32–40 weeks  
**Suggested hours:** 280–360

Focus on:

- implicit meaning, attitude, humor, and complex arguments;
- precise vocabulary and control of formal/informal style;
- fluent participation in demanding discussions;
- synthesis of several sources;
- clear, flexible, well-structured writing and speaking.

Main projects:

- a 15-minute evidence-based presentation and Q&A;
- compare and synthesize three sources;
- participate in a 30-minute complex discussion;
- write and revise a 300–350 word report or essay.

Detailed plan: [`levels/B2-C1.md`](levels/B2-C1.md)

Keep every milestone file. Comparing old and new work makes progress visible.

## Monthly rhythm

Use a four-week cycle at every level:

- **Week 1:** new language and guided practice;
- **Week 2:** new language and more listening/reading;
- **Week 3:** communication project and feedback;
- **Week 4:** less new material, more review, testing, and correction.

At the end of each month:

1. total your focused study hours;
2. compare your output with the previous month;
3. identify one weak skill;
4. give that skill two extra sessions next month;
5. remove any resource you consistently avoid;
6. choose the next real-world project.

## Personal specialization

From B1, reserve 20% of study time for your reason for learning English.

- **Work:** meetings, emails, presentations, technical vocabulary.
- **University:** lectures, note-taking, essays, academic vocabulary.
- **Travel/social:** spontaneous conversation, accents, practical situations.
- **Exams:** timed tasks and official exam format.
- **Technology:** documentation, issue reports, demos, and technical discussion.

General English remains 80% until B2. A narrow specialist vocabulary cannot
replace broad language ability.
