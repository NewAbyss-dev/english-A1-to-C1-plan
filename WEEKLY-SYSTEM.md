# Weekly Study System

## The standard week: 10 hours

Use six study days. Change the clock times, but keep the balance.

| Activity | Weekly target |
|---|---:|
| Main course/book and grammar | 2 h 30 min |
| Listening | 1 h 30 min |
| Speaking and pronunciation | 2 h |
| Reading | 1 h |
| Writing | 1 h |
| Vocabulary review | 1 h |
| Weekly review/test | 1 h |

Some activities train two skills. A spoken summary of a podcast counts as
listening and speaking, but count the minutes only once in your total.

## A focused 90-minute session

1. **Recall — 10 minutes**
   - Without notes, say what you learned yesterday.
   - Review due vocabulary.
2. **Input — 25 minutes**
   - Complete part of your book, a listening lesson, or a short text.
3. **Language focus — 15 minutes**
   - Choose one grammar pattern, five phrases, or one pronunciation feature.
4. **Output — 25 minutes**
   - Speak or write using the new language.
5. **Feedback — 10 minutes**
   - Check answers or request corrections.
   - Record no more than three important errors.
6. **Plan — 5 minutes**
   - Write the next action in your English Log.

Use 25-minute focus blocks with 5-minute breaks if 90 minutes feels difficult.

## Listening method

Use audio that you can partly understand.

1. Listen once without text. Identify topic, speakers, and main idea.
2. Listen again. Write key words and answer questions.
3. Check the transcript. Highlight only useful unknown phrases.
4. Replay difficult 10–20 second sections.
5. Shadow 3–5 sentences: listen, pause, imitate rhythm and stress.
6. Give a spoken summary without the transcript.

At A1–A2, use short learner audio. At B1, mix learner and authentic audio. At
B2–C1, authentic audio should become the majority.

## Speaking method

Every speaking session needs a purpose:

- tell a story;
- explain a process;
- describe and compare;
- solve a problem;
- express and defend an opinion;
- summarize something you heard or read.

Use the **3-2-1 fluency drill** once a week:

1. Speak about one topic for 3 minutes.
2. Speak about it again for 2 minutes.
3. Speak about it a third time for 1 minute.

Do not script every word. After speaking, note:

- one thing you communicated well;
- three repeated mistakes;
- three phrases you needed but did not know.

Repeat the topic later with the corrections.

## Reading method

Choose material where you understand:

- around 95% of words for relaxed extensive reading;
- around 85–95% for careful intensive study.

For extensive reading, keep moving and guess. For intensive reading:

1. predict the topic;
2. read once for the main idea;
3. read again for structure and detail;
4. collect 5–8 useful phrases;
5. summarize the text orally or in writing;
6. react: agree, disagree, connect, or ask a question.

## Writing method

Write at least once a week.

1. Decide the reader and purpose.
2. Make a short outline.
3. Write the first draft without stopping for every error.
4. Check content and organization.
5. Check grammar, vocabulary, spelling, and punctuation.
6. Get feedback.
7. Rewrite the text.
8. Add repeated errors to an error log.

Do not ask a tool to rewrite everything immediately. First ask it to:

> Mark my errors, explain the three most important patterns in simple English,
> and let me correct them before showing a model answer.

## Vocabulary method

Learn phrases, not isolated translations.

Weak card:

> decision = [translation]

Stronger card:

> make a difficult decision  
> I had to make a difficult decision about ___.

Daily limits:

- A1–A2: 5–10 new items;
- B1: 8–12 new items;
- B2–C1: 10–15 new items, mostly phrases and collocations.

Review cards due today. Do not add new cards when reviews are out of control.
Delete cards that are unclear, rare, or never useful.

## Pronunciation method

Accent is not the main goal. Clear, comfortable speech is.

Work on:

1. sounds that change meaning;
2. word stress;
3. sentence stress;
4. weak forms and connected speech;
5. rhythm and pauses;
6. intonation for meaning and attitude.

Record one short imitation each week. Compare it with the original for rhythm,
stress, and clarity—not for having an identical voice.

## Feedback ladder

Use the most useful feedback you can access:

1. self-check with answer keys, transcripts, and recordings;
2. automated grammar or pronunciation feedback;
3. exchange partner;
4. teacher or tutor;
5. official practice tests at milestones.

Automated feedback can be wrong. Confirm surprising advice with a reliable
dictionary, grammar reference, or teacher.

## Build a good habit

- Attach English to a fixed event: “After breakfast, I study for 25 minutes.”
- Prepare the exact lesson the night before.
- Keep headphones, book, notebook, and recorder ready.
- Track focused minutes, not time sitting at a desk.
- Stop a session with a clear next step.
- Reward consistency each week.
