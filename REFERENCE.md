# Language and Learning Reference

This is a checklist, not a sequence of lessons. Study grammar and vocabulary in
context through your course and communication tasks.

## Grammar by target level

### Toward A2

- `be`, `have`, present simple, present continuous;
- basic questions, negatives, pronouns, possessives;
- articles, plurals, countable/uncountable nouns;
- place/time prepositions, frequency adverbs;
- past simple; basic future with `going to` and `will`;
- comparatives/superlatives;
- `can`, `should`, `must`, `have to`;
- basic connectors: `and`, `but`, `because`, `so`, `then`.

### Toward B1

- present perfect and past simple;
- past continuous and narrative tense combinations;
- future forms and time clauses;
- first and second conditionals;
- common passive forms;
- relative clauses;
- gerunds and infinitives;
- reported statements/questions;
- modal meanings: ability, advice, obligation, possibility, deduction;
- linkers for sequence, contrast, cause, result, and example.

### Toward B2

- tense/aspect choices across longer discourse;
- past modals and advanced modality;
- passive variations and causatives;
- third and mixed conditionals;
- advanced relative clauses;
- reported language;
- articles, determiners, and quantifiers in complex contexts;
- participle clauses;
- emphasis and inversion for recognition/use;
- cohesive reference across paragraphs.

### Toward C1

- precise tense, aspect, modality, and article choices;
- ellipsis, substitution, clefting, inversion, and emphasis;
- complex clause combinations;
- participle and reduced clauses;
- nominalization and information density;
- hedging and stance;
- punctuation for meaning and flow;
- deliberate grammar choices for register and rhetorical effect.

Knowing a rule is not enough. You “own” it when you can use it spontaneously,
notice it in real input, and correct your frequent mistakes.

## Vocabulary priorities

Learn:

1. high-frequency words;
2. useful phrases and sentence frames;
3. collocations (`make a decision`, not only `decision`);
4. words for your life, work, interests, and current project;
5. word families (`decide`, `decision`, `decisive`);
6. register and connotation at higher levels.

Avoid:

- long unreviewed lists;
- rare synonyms chosen only to sound advanced;
- cards with no context;
- learning every unknown word in a text;
- treating an estimated word count as proof of CEFR level.

## Essential communication frames

### Keep a conversation moving

- “Could you say that again more slowly?”
- “What does ___ mean?”
- “Do you mean that ___?”
- “Let me think for a moment.”
- “I don't know the exact word, but it is a thing/person/place that ___.”

### Organize speech

- “First, … / The main point is …”
- “For example, …”
- “On the other hand, …”
- “The reason is that …”
- “As a result, …”
- “To sum up, …”

### Discuss at B1–B2

- “In my view, …”
- “I partly agree because …”
- “One advantage/disadvantage is …”
- “That depends on …”
- “A possible solution would be …”

### Add nuance at B2–C1

- “There is some evidence to suggest that …”
- “That may be true to some extent; however, …”
- “A key distinction is …”
- “This raises the question of whether …”
- “I would argue that …, although …”
- “Could you clarify what you mean by …?”

## Error log

Use four columns:

| My sentence | Better sentence | Reason | New personal example |
|---|---|---|---|
| I am agree. | I agree. | `agree` is a verb | I agree with this idea. |

Review only repeated or important errors. Each week:

1. choose three patterns;
2. make five correct personal examples for each;
3. use them in a short speaking/writing task;
4. check again one week later.

## Translation

Translation is a tool, not a failure.

- At A1–A2, use it to understand instructions and check meaning.
- First try to understand from examples and context.
- Translate phrases or full ideas, not every isolated word.
- Always check pronunciation and an English example.
- From B1, use simple English definitions more often.
- At B2–C1, compare nuances across several authentic examples.

## Using AI carefully

Useful requests:

- “Have an A2 conversation with me about shopping. Ask one question at a time.”
- “Correct only errors that block meaning or happen repeatedly.”
- “Explain this grammar in A1 English and give five examples about daily life.”
- “Give me a B1 role-play. Do not write my answers.”
- “Evaluate this using the rubric in `ASSESSMENT.md`; quote evidence.”

Do not let AI:

- write all your texts before you try;
- translate everything automatically;
- replace real listening with text chat;
- give so many corrections that you cannot practice them;
- become your only source of pronunciation or factual information.
