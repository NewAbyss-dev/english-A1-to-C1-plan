# Assessment and Level Checks

CEFR levels describe what you can **do**. Vocabulary size, finished books, app
streaks, and grammar scores are useful evidence, but none proves a level alone.

## Test every 4 weeks

Use one 60–90 minute review:

1. complete one listening and one reading activity at your target level;
2. record an unprepared speaking task;
3. write a timed text;
4. review your error log;
5. score consistency, not perfection.

Save every result with the date.

## Simple scoring rubric

Score speaking and writing from 0 to 5 in each area.

| Score | Meaning |
|---:|---|
| 0 | No meaningful attempt |
| 1 | Very limited; message often unclear |
| 2 | Partial success; frequent breakdowns |
| 3 | Task completed; limitations are clear but meaning is generally understandable |
| 4 | Good control for the target level; mistakes rarely block meaning |
| 5 | Strong, flexible performance at the target level |

Areas:

- **Task:** Did you answer fully and appropriately?
- **Organization:** Are ideas connected and easy to follow?
- **Range:** Do vocabulary and structures fit the target level?
- **Accuracy:** How often do errors confuse the listener/reader?
- **Fluency** (speaking): Can you continue without excessive pauses?
- **Pronunciation** (speaking): Is speech clear and appropriately stressed?

For writing, replace fluency/pronunciation with:

- **Cohesion:** Do sentences and paragraphs connect well?
- **Mechanics:** Are spelling and punctuation controlled?

Ask a teacher or capable reviewer to score milestone work when possible.

## A2 milestone

Complete without a script:

- **Listening:** understand the main point and key details in short, clear
  everyday messages.
- **Reading:** understand short messages, notices, menus, schedules, and a simple
  personal story.
- **Speaking:** speak for 3 minutes about your life, a past event, and a future
  plan; complete two practical role-plays.
- **Writing:** write an 80–120 word message or story.

Advance when reading/listening are around 70–80% on several level-appropriate
tasks and speaking/writing average at least 3, with no area below 2.

## B1 milestone

- **Listening:** identify main points and important details in clear speech about
  work, study, travel, or interests.
- **Reading:** understand straightforward articles and a graded reader.
- **Speaking:** tell a 5-minute story, give reasons for opinions, and hold a
  15-minute familiar conversation.
- **Writing:** produce a connected 140–180 word email, story, or opinion.
- **Project:** explain a real problem and recommend a solution using two sources.

Use the same threshold across several tasks, not one lucky result.

## B2 milestone

- **Listening:** follow the argument in extended standard-language speech and
  understand most factual media on familiar and some unfamiliar topics.
- **Reading:** identify viewpoint, evidence, and detail in complex articles.
- **Speaking:** present for 10 minutes, answer questions, and discuss an abstract
  issue with reasonable spontaneity.
- **Writing:** produce a clear 220–280 word essay/report with developed reasons,
  examples, and suitable register.
- **Project:** synthesize three sources and defend a conclusion.

At B2, repeated basic errors should not regularly cause misunderstanding.

## C1 milestone

- **Listening:** follow long complex speech and infer attitude or implicit
  meaning, including in less familiar topics.
- **Reading:** understand long complex factual/literary texts and recognize
  style, purpose, and argument.
- **Speaking:** give a 15-minute precise presentation, answer unexpected
  questions, and participate flexibly in a 30-minute complex discussion.
- **Writing:** create a well-structured 300–350 word synthesis/report/essay with
  controlled register, cohesion, qualification, and precise vocabulary.
- **Project:** complete the capstone in [`levels/B2-C1.md`](levels/B2-C1.md).

C1 does not require zero mistakes. It requires effective, flexible control in
demanding communication.

## What to do after a failed check

Do not repeat the entire level.

1. Name the weakest skill and one specific problem.
2. Find three examples from your work.
3. Set a two-week repair plan with four focused sessions each week.
4. Practice with new material.
5. repeat a parallel task, not the memorized original.

Examples:

- “listening is bad” → “I lose details when speakers use weak forms”;
- “grammar is bad” → “I confuse past simple and present perfect in stories”;
- “speaking is bad” → “I pause because I lack phrases for giving examples.”

## Formal assessment

Online placement tests are approximate and often test receptive knowledge more
than speaking/writing. For high-stakes proof:

- confirm which certificate and minimum score are accepted;
- use the test provider's official sample materials;
- get writing/speaking evaluated against official criteria;
- book a recognized test when practice scores are consistently high enough.

Use the official [CEFR self-assessment grid](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/table-2-cefr-3.3-common-reference-levels-self-assessment-grid)
to compare your abilities across all skills.
