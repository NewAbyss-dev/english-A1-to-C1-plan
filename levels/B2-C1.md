# Phase 4: B2 → C1

**Length:** 32–40 weeks  
**Work:** 280–360 hours  
**Main course:** advanced reference/course plus mostly authentic English

## Exit abilities

By the end, you can usually:

- follow long, complex speech even when structure is not explicit;
- understand attitude, implication, humor, and changes of register;
- speak fluently and flexibly for social, academic, or professional purposes;
- formulate ideas precisely and respond skillfully to other speakers;
- read complex factual and literary texts and recognize style;
- write clear, well-structured, detailed texts on complex subjects.

## Block 1 — Diagnose and refine (weeks 1–8)

Build a personal syllabus from evidence:

1. collect three recordings and three texts;
2. mark repeated grammar, vocabulary, pronunciation, and organization problems;
3. rank problems by effect on communication;
4. choose the top five patterns;
5. deliberately practice and retest one pattern each week.

Advanced language areas:

- tense, aspect, modality, and article choice in context;
- complex clauses, ellipsis, substitution, and reference;
- cleft sentences, inversion, and emphasis;
- nominalization and dense formal language;
- punctuation and information flow.

Do not study advanced grammar only because it looks difficult. Prioritize what
appears in your input or improves your output.

## Block 2 — Precision and register (weeks 9–16)

Focus:

- strong collocations and lexical bundles;
- nuanced near-synonyms and connotation;
- hedging, diplomacy, persuasion, and disagreement;
- formal, neutral, informal, and conversational styles;
- metaphor, idiomatic language, and humor in context.

Weekly contrast exercise:

1. explain one idea to a friend;
2. explain it in a workplace meeting;
3. explain it in a formal report;
4. compare vocabulary, directness, and sentence structure.

## Block 3 — Complex comprehension (weeks 17–24)

Use long-form sources:

- lectures, documentaries, panel discussions, and unscripted podcasts;
- analysis, long-form journalism, essays, and quality fiction;
- several speakers and a range of accents.

For one source each week, analyze:

- thesis and structure;
- evidence and assumptions;
- speaker/writer attitude;
- implicit meaning;
- rhetorical techniques;
- unfamiliar language worth learning.

Then give a concise summary and a critical response without copying the source.

## Block 4 — High-level production (weeks 25–32)

Speaking:

- negotiate, persuade, facilitate, and disagree diplomatically;
- give 10–15 minute structured talks with minimal notes;
- answer unexpected questions;
- reformulate when misunderstood;
- participate in fast multi-person discussion.

Writing:

- synthesize several sources;
- control paragraph flow and cohesion;
- use evidence and qualification;
- edit for concision, precision, and register;
- produce essays, proposals, reports, reviews, or professional documents.

Complete one major speaking and one major writing task every two weeks.

## Consolidation or exam preparation (weeks 33–40)

For general C1:

- spend 80–90% of input time with authentic English;
- maintain broad topics and one specialization;
- repeat weak assessment tasks;
- seek detailed human feedback at least twice a month.

For a certificate:

1. identify the exact exam and required score;
2. use its official handbook and sample tests;
3. learn timing and task criteria;
4. complete timed sections;
5. analyze errors by category;
6. complete at least two full practice tests under realistic conditions.

Exam technique does not replace C1 ability. Add it after building the skills.

## Final capstone

Choose a complex question relevant to your life or work.

1. Use at least three credible English sources in different formats.
2. Write a 300–350 word synthesis with your reasoned conclusion.
3. Give a 15-minute presentation with clear structure and minimal notes.
4. Answer 10 minutes of unprepared questions.
5. Revise the text and repeat part of the presentation after feedback.
6. Compare this work with your A1 baseline.

## Weekly targets

- 4–5 hours of complex authentic input;
- 2–3 hours of discussion, presentation, or speaking analysis;
- one substantial first draft and one revision;
- one source-synthesis task;
- targeted vocabulary and error-log review;
- two pronunciation/rhetoric sessions;
- frequent use of English in real work, study, or relationships.

## C1 maintenance

Reaching C1 is not the end. Maintain it with:

- regular long-form reading and listening;
- weekly meaningful conversation;
- monthly substantial writing;
- continued work in your specialization;
- occasional recordings and feedback;
- new subjects that force you beyond familiar vocabulary.

Complete the C1 milestone in [`../ASSESSMENT.md`](../ASSESSMENT.md). If you need
formal proof, take the recognized test required by your institution.
