# Phase 2: A2 → B1

**Length:** 20–24 weeks  
**Work:** 180–240 hours  
**Main course:** one complete B1 course or structured B1 resource

## Exit abilities

By the end, you can usually:

- understand the main points of clear speech on familiar subjects;
- manage most situations while traveling;
- enter an unprepared conversation about everyday topics;
- narrate events and explain plans, opinions, hopes, and reasons;
- read straightforward factual texts and a graded reader;
- write connected texts of about 140–180 words.

## Block 1 — Strong stories (weeks 1–5)

Topics: travel, experiences, relationships, education, work.

Language:

- past simple, past continuous, and present perfect;
- present perfect with `for`, `since`, `just`, `already`, and `yet`;
- narrative sequence and common irregular verbs;
- adjective and preposition combinations.

Outputs:

- tell a five-minute personal story;
- interview someone about an experience;
- write a 150-word travel story;
- read one short graded reader and retell each chapter.

## Block 2 — Plans, rules, and problems (weeks 6–10)

Topics: health, money, services, technology, environment.

Language:

- future forms: `will`, `going to`, and present continuous;
- modals for advice, obligation, possibility, and deduction;
- first and second conditionals;
- verbs followed by infinitives or gerunds.

Outputs:

- solve a travel or service problem through role-play;
- give advice about health or study habits;
- explain a personal five-year plan;
- discuss “What would you do if …?” questions.

## Block 3 — Explain and compare (weeks 11–15)

Topics: media, culture, products, cities, lifestyles.

Language:

- comparatives, superlatives, and modifiers;
- relative clauses;
- passive voice in common present/past forms;
- linking contrast, cause, result, and example.

Outputs:

- compare two products or cities for five minutes;
- explain how something is made or done;
- summarize a news story written for learners;
- write a balanced opinion with reasons and examples.

## Block 4 — Independent communication (weeks 16–20)

Topics: goals, society, work situations, entertainment.

Language:

- reported statements and questions;
- question tags and indirect questions;
- phrasal verbs and common collocations;
- paragraph organization and reference words.

Outputs:

- hold a 15-minute conversation with limited preparation;
- give a five-minute presentation and answer questions;
- write a formal request and an informal message;
- finish a second graded reader and write a review.

## Consolidation (optional weeks 21–24)

Use authentic English for 25–30% of input:

- short podcast segments;
- simple news reports;
- interviews with clear speakers;
- short articles on familiar subjects.

Do not expect 100% understanding. Identify the main idea, important details,
speaker purpose, and a small number of useful phrases.

Final project:

> Choose a real problem in your life or community. Read or listen to two simple
> sources, explain the problem, compare two solutions, and recommend one. Give a
> 5–7 minute presentation and write 150–180 words.

## Weekly targets

- 2–3 hours of structured course work;
- 2 hours of listening, including one transcript study;
- 2 hours of speaking, including one live conversation;
- 60–90 minutes of reading;
- one 140–180 word text;
- 8–12 useful new phrases on most study days;
- one episode/chapter of extensive input.

## Exit check

Complete the B1 milestone in [`../ASSESSMENT.md`](../ASSESSMENT.md). At B1 you
should begin using English to learn about interests—not only learning English.
