# Start Here

Do not try to plan the next two years today. Set up one week and begin.

## Day 1: prepare your system (60–90 minutes)

### 1. Write your reason

Complete this sentence:

> I want C1 English because __________________________________________.

Then write one practical 90-day goal:

> In 90 days, I want to ______________________________________________.

Good examples:

- have a five-minute conversation about my life;
- understand an easy 10-minute video;
- finish my A1 book and pass an A2 practice test.

### 2. Record your starting point

Do these tasks without help. Keep the results.

1. **Speaking:** record yourself for two minutes. Introduce yourself and describe
   a normal day.
2. **Writing:** write 100 words about your life, interests, and plans.
3. **Listening:** complete one activity at your current level on
   [British Council LearnEnglish](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/listening).
4. **Reading:** complete one activity at your current level on
   [British Council LearnEnglish](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/reading).
5. Optional: take one online level test. Treat the result as an estimate, not a
   final truth.

Save the recording, text, scores, and date in a folder named `baseline`.

### 3. Prepare four tools

Use simple tools you will actually open:

- your **A1 course book** and its audio;
- a notebook or digital note named **English Log**;
- a spaced-repetition app or paper cards;
- a phone or computer voice recorder.

Do not buy many courses. One main book is enough.

### 4. Map your A1 book

Count the units and choose a finish date. Use:

> units ÷ study weeks = units per week

Example: 12 units ÷ 12 weeks = 1 unit each week.

For every book unit, complete this loop:

1. Preview the title, pictures, and lesson goal.
2. Listen once without reading.
3. Listen again with the transcript.
4. Study the grammar and examples.
5. Learn 10–15 useful words or phrases.
6. Complete the exercises and check them.
7. Say a 1–2 minute summary using the lesson language.
8. Write 6–10 sentences about your own life.
9. Review the unit after 1 day, 7 days, and 30 days.

Do not copy every new word. Keep words that are common or useful to **you**.

## Your first week

Use the recommended 10-hour schedule:

| Day | Work | Time |
|---|---|---:|
| Monday | Book lesson + vocabulary + read aloud | 90 min |
| Tuesday | Book lesson + listening + short speaking | 90 min |
| Wednesday | Grammar review + writing + corrections | 90 min |
| Thursday | Book lesson + pronunciation + speaking | 90 min |
| Friday | Reading + listening + vocabulary review | 90 min |
| Saturday | Conversation/project + weekly test/review | 150 min |
| Sunday | Rest; optional enjoyable English | 0–30 min |

See [`WEEKLY-SYSTEM.md`](WEEKLY-SYSTEM.md) for exact session instructions.

## If you struggle

Use these rules:

- **The audio is too fast:** use a transcript, reduce speed, listen in 10–20
  second pieces, then return to normal speed.
- **You forget words:** learn fewer words, use complete phrases, and review them
  on several different days.
- **You cannot speak:** prepare five sentence starters, speak alone first, then
  speak with a partner.
- **Grammar is confusing:** learn one useful pattern and make ten personal
  examples. Do not read five explanations.
- **You feel embarrassed:** record privately. Delete the recording if you want.
  The goal is practice.
- **You missed a week:** restart with a minimum day. Do not “pay back” every
  missed hour.

## Find speaking practice

Start in week 1, even with basic English:

- two 15-minute self-recording sessions each week;
- one 30-minute conversation each week at A1–A2;
- two 30–45 minute conversations each week from B1;
- feedback from a tutor, teacher, exchange partner, or careful AI tool.

Ask a partner to correct only **three important repeated errors** after you
finish speaking. Too many interruptions reduce fluency.

## When to move forward

Do not wait to feel “ready.” At the end of each phase, complete the test in
[`ASSESSMENT.md`](ASSESSMENT.md). Advance when:

- you achieve about 70–80% in level-appropriate listening and reading tasks;
- you complete the speaking and writing projects;
- your most important grammar and vocabulary are usable, not just recognizable;
- no single skill is far below the others.

If you do not pass, repeat only the weak areas for 2–4 weeks and test again.
